Audited 26 real user messages from the earlier build session against the persisted claims.

CAPTURED WELL — propositional statements, the 'use X / not Y' shape: explicit rejections (x2 compressed images, x3 commit-message anchoring, x4 Desktop-as-MCP-only) all with reasons; the d3->d4 reversal with its reason; architecture (d1, d2).

MISSED — framing statements, which are not syntactically decisions:
  - 'The state engine is the moat' — said verbatim in the same sentence that produced d3. Zero of 116 claims mention 'moat' or 'state engine'. It survived only in the assistant's separate auto-memory, i.e. outside the product.
  - 'your call, I gave you full ownership' — a standing instruction about authority; nowhere.

Why it matters: framing statements determine what is worth building, so they are the highest-leverage content in the corpus, and they are exactly what a decision-shaped extractor cannot see. 40 supersessions of bookkeeping survived while the thesis did not.

Hypothesis needing a larger denominator, not yet established: recall is high for propositions and near zero for framing. Next pass would be this session's transcript (~3,215 lines, ~50 capture events).

Deliberately NOT captured here: the moat thesis itself. It is the owner's sentence and reconstructing it from memory would repeat the authority error this audit exists to find — it needs their wording.
