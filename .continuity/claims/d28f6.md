58 of the ~110 tracked files are .continuity/claims. Publishing them exposes the project's own candid record: six of its own bugs (rk1-rk6, each marked resolved with the commit that fixed it), the rejected logo directions, the superseded architecture (d3->d4, d11->d22rb), and blunt engineering assessments. Judged a strength for a developer tool rather than a liability — every respected OSS project has a public tracker containing worse, and the lineage is better documentation of the data model than any synthetic example.

No partial option was viable: a curated subset breaks lineage and defeats the determinism that makes the resume trustworthy. Verified before approval that no third-party or customer data appears in any claim, all four cited SHAs resolve post-rewrite, and neither email address is named anywhere. Do not re-open this; if anything, it is the strongest demo the project has.
